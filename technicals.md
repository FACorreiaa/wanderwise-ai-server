This is a fantastic and absolutely critical business strategy question. Your intuition is spot on—you're wrestling with the classic freemium dilemma: **how much value do you give away to hook a user versus how much do you hold back to monetize?**

Let's break it down.

### Short Answer: Yes, It Is ESSENTIAL.

Yes, you should **absolutely** allow non-logged-in users to use core AI features. Not doing so in 2024 for a consumer application is a critical mistake. Hiding your core value proposition behind a login wall is like a restaurant putting a blindfold on you before you can read the menu.

---

### The "Why": The Psychology of User Acquisition

Allowing anonymous access is the cornerstone of modern product-led growth. Here’s why it works:

1.  **Frictionless Discovery (The "Wow" Moment):** The single most important goal for a new user is to get them to an "Aha!" or "Wow!" moment as fast as possible. A user who lands on your page should be able to type "3-hour morning walk in Lisbon for history buffs" and see a magical, personalized result within seconds. That's your hook. A mandatory sign-up is a wall of friction that kills this magic before it can even happen.

2.  **Product as the Salesperson:** You let the quality of your AI results sell the product for you. A user who gets a genuinely useful itinerary is infinitely more likely to sign up to save it than a user who is just promised a useful itinerary on the other side of a registration form.

3.  **SEO and Discoverability:** Content behind a login wall is invisible to Google. If your AI can generate a public page for "best tapas restaurants in Barcelona for under €50," that page can be indexed and attract organic search traffic. This is a massive, free user acquisition channel.

4.  **Virality and Sharing:** A user should be able to generate a cool itinerary and share the link with a friend. That friend must be able to see it instantly without signing up. This is how products grow.

---

### The "How": Your Levers for Monetization

This is the answer to your second, crucial question: "What would stop users from never logging in or paying?"

You create a "value ladder." You give immediate value for free, but you make the experience so much better when a user commits. Here are your levers, from softest to hardest:

#### **Lever 1: Usage Limits & Rate Limiting (The Soft Wall)**

This is your most basic and effective tool for anonymous users.

- **Implementation:** An anonymous user (based on IP address or a browser cookie) gets **3-5 free AI searches per day/week**.
- **The Psychology:** This does two things:
  1.  It gives them enough runway to experience the "wow" moment.
  2.  It establishes that the AI feature is valuable and has a cost. When they hit the limit, the message isn't "Go Away," it's "This is so valuable people pay for it. **Sign up for free to get more.**"

#### **Lever 2: Feature Gating (The Path to Premium)**

This is where you clearly define what is free versus what is a premium, power-user feature.

**The most powerful feature to gate is PERSISTENCE.**

- **Anonymous User:** Can generate an itinerary. Sees an amazing result.
- **The Trigger:** A prominent button says **"Save this Itinerary."**
- **The Action:** Clicking that button prompts them: **"Sign up for a free account to save and access your trips anywhere."**

This is your primary conversion tool from anonymous to a logged-in free user. People have a natural aversion to losing something they've created or found.

**Other Features to Gate:**

| Feature                            | Anonymous User | Logged-in FREE User          | Logged-in PREMIUM User            |
| ---------------------------------- | -------------- | ---------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| **AI Searches**                    | 3-5 per day    | 20-30 per day                | **Unlimited**                     |
| **Saving Itineraries/POIs**        | ❌ No          | Limited (e.g., 1 itinerary)  | **Unlimited**                     |
| **Personalization (Memory)**       | ❌ No          | ✅ Yes (remembers interests) | **✅ Enhanced (Learns from you)** |
| **Advanced Filters (niche, etc.)** | ❌ No          | ❌ No                        | **✅ Yes**                        |
| **Itinerary Download (PDF/MD)**    | ❌ No          | ❌ No                        | **✅ Yes**                        |
| **Speech-to-Text Input**           | ❌ No          | ❌ No                        | **✅ Yes**                        |
| **24/7 Personalised Agent**        | ❌ No          | ❌ No                        | **✅ Yes**                        |
| **Ad-Free Experience**             | Ads Shown      | Ads Shown                    | **✅ Ad-Free**                    |

#### **Lever 3: Degraded Experience (The Quality Ladder)**

The quality of the AI's output itself can be a lever.

- **Anonymous User:** Gets a great, but somewhat generic result.
- **Logged-in Free User:** The AI can use their stated interests. "Here is a history-focused itinerary for Lisbon."
- **Premium User:** The AI can use their _learned behavior_ and context. "I see you previously enjoyed the 'hidden gems' in Porto and you often save craft beer pubs. Here is a history-focused itinerary for Lisbon that includes two off-the-beaten-path viewpoints and ends near a top-rated craft beer bar."

The core product gets demonstrably smarter and more _hyper-personalized_ the more the user invests in it, creating a powerful incentive to upgrade.

### Your New User Funnel Strategy

Based on this, your user journey should look like this:

1.  **The Hook (Anonymous):** User lands on the page, makes 1-3 free queries, and is wowed by the result. They see they can't save it.
2.  **The Conversion (Sign-up):** They click "Save" and create a free account to avoid losing their itinerary.
3.  **The Habit (Free User):** Now logged in, they create their one free itinerary. They use the app more, bumping into the premium feature walls (e.g., "Upgrade to download as PDF," "Upgrade for advanced filters").
4.  **The Monetization (Premium):** A user planning a real trip gets frustrated by the limitations (e.g., can't save a separate itinerary for Day 2) and decides the small monthly subscription is worth it for a stress-free planning experience.

By implementing this tiered strategy, you get the best of both worlds: a powerful, frictionless engine for user acquisition and clear, compelling reasons for users to log in and eventually pay.
